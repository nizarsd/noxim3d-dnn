\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}
The major bottleneck of DNN accelerators is communication. Dataflow
accelerators schedule data reuse to reduce traffic~\cite{chenEyerissSpatialArchitecture2016,szeEfficientProcessingDeep2017}.
In IMC accelerators, weights are fixed in crossbar
tiles~\cite{shafieeISAACConvolutionalNeural2016,chiPRIMENovelProcessinginMemory2016,longFerroelectricFETBasedProcessinginMemory2019} which places the remaining traffic (activations and partial sums) on the on-chip
network~\cite{nabavinejadOverviewEfficientInterconnection2020}. Krishnan et
al.~\cite{krishnanImpactOnchipInterconnect2022} explore NoC-mesh and
NoC-tree topologies across DNNs, motivated by the inability of a
point-to-point interconnect to carry IMC traffic, and drive the NoC with
steady per-pair injection rates and no phase structure, the traffic model
this work adopts and extends with phase windows. Full layers are mapped to tiles, so the NoC sees no partial-sum traffic, and placement is fixed. The same group models IMC and its NoC end to
end~\cite{krishnanSIAMChipletbasedScalable2021} and synthesizes a NoC per
DNN~\cite{mandalLatencyOptimizedReconfigurableNoC2020}, taking topology as
the variable, fixing routing and selection. 
On 3D NoCs, TTNNM~\cite{liTTNNMThermalTrafficAware2024} and
TEFLON~\cite{narangTEFLONThermallyEfficient2024} fix the packing and optimize
mapping or fabric. Adaptive routing and selection were developed largely on
synthetic
traffic~\cite{dahirHighlyAdaptiveDeadlockFree2013,makDPnetworkOptimalDynamic2009}.

These works focus on optimizing one layer with the others held fixed: packing
for utilization or traffic volume, mapping for hops or heat, routing for a
given placement. None studies how a choice at one layer changes what the next can
achieve, which this work shows to have a major effect:
the minimum-traffic packing is the wrong one for delay, mapping matters only in a
regime that packing decides, and what selection yields depends on both.
